Money

Payments and cash

Use local currency.
Choose EUR in France and CHF in Switzerland. Decline any offer to charge or convert the transaction in USD.
Use bank-operated ATMs.
Avoid exchange counters and standalone tourist ATMs. At an ATM, decline its currency conversion and let the card network perform the exchange.
Use credit cards for routine purchases.
Carry modest incidental cash, plus the separately secured cash required for the two Strasbourg driver days.
Keep a backup payment method separate.
Do not store every card and all cash in the same wallet or bag.
Trains

Rail rules

Use the operating app for live information.
SBB Mobile is the primary source for Swiss platforms, delays and connection changes; SNCF Connect is useful for French operations.
Board the correct train before optimizing the seat.
If a connection is short, get aboard first. Class markings are shown outside the coaches.
Prepare before the station stop.
Gather bags and move toward the door several minutes before a short connection.
Keep tickets and discount cards offline.
Each traveler should be able to display the required ticket, Half Fare Travelcard and identification.
Follow the booked train restrictions.
Saver and international tickets may be valid only on the listed services. If a railway delay breaks the connection, use the app or staff for the next valid routing.
Tickets and weather

Sightseeing rules

Keep fixed reservations limited.
Timed attractions anchor the day; the surrounding itinerary remains flexible.
Weather decides the Swiss mountain day.
Jungfraujoch is purchased 1–2 days ahead only with excellent visibility. Männlichen and Mürren are bought the day before or day of travel. Lake Brienz is chosen 1–3 days ahead.
Weather also decides the cruises.
Book Vedettes du Pont Neuf and Batorama about 2–5 days ahead after checking the forecast.
Free does not always mean no planning.
Notre-Dame is free but an optional timed slot can reduce uncertainty. Other free sites may still have changing opening hours or security lines.
Technology

Phone and document rules

Maintain two forms of access.
Keep documents in the relevant app and as an offline PDF or screenshot in Google Drive.
Download maps before departure.
Offline maps reduce dependence on roaming and mountain coverage.
Keep phones charged.
Start full, carry a power bank and avoid using the only phone as the sole repository for both travelers.
Safety and pace

Daily conduct

Secure valuables.
Use front pockets or closed bags in crowds and on transit. Keep phones, wallets and passports off café tables.
Carry passports deliberately.
Use the hotel safe when originals are unnecessary; keep secure access when border, rail or identification checks may require them.
Choose quality over quantity.
The itinerary is a framework, not a completion requirement. A longer meal, park visit or neighborhood discovery can replace an optional stop.
Use the easier return when energy drops.
Taxis and transit are tools, not failures. Save walking energy for the parts of the day that matter most.
Health

Medical readiness

Carry travel medical insurance details.
Keep the policy number, emergency assistance line, coverage summary and claims instructions accessible offline.
Keep medications in carry-on luggage.
Bring sufficient supply for the trip, a small buffer and a written medication list using generic names.
Know the escalation path.
For urgent symptoms, use local emergency services; for non-emergency care, contact the insurer’s assistance line and the hotel front desk.
